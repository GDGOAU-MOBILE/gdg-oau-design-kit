# Partner's-eye review

Before delivering, read the whole piece as the person who receives it. For a sponsor pitch, imagine the
partner's sponsorship manager, their finance lead and their compliance officer reading it in turn. Find
everything that could annoy, confuse, worry or embarrass them, fix it, and tell the user what you changed.

## Sponsorship manager

- [ ] Does any line tell us what to do instead of asking?
- [ ] Is our leader named with respect (title and surname) and invited, not assumed?
- [ ] Is anything presented as agreed that we have not agreed to (keynote, staff, booths)?
- [ ] Does the deadline clash with anything we said about our timing or budget cycle?
- [ ] Are we compared unfavourably, or does a "fact" about us read like a complaint?
- [ ] Do they claim we are "first" when they are pitching several companies?
- [ ] Are extra costs (booths, logistics) sprung on us after the main ask?

## Finance lead

- [ ] Do the numbers agree on every page? Multiply anything that can be multiplied (people × unit cost)
      and check it fits inside the stated budget split.
- [ ] Are tiers, prices and what each includes consistent with the source document?
- [ ] Is it clear how payment works and that spending is documented?

## Compliance and brand

- [ ] Is any sign-up, purchase or data sharing made a condition of attending? It must be voluntary.
- [ ] Are personal data (account numbers, phone numbers, CVs) collected or shown? CVs only with consent.
- [ ] Are our logo and product names used correctly and only where relevant?
- [ ] Are outcomes overpromised ("800+ new customers")?

## Students and the university

- [ ] Would a student feel pressured or used as a sales target?
- [ ] Are OAU, its halls and its people named correctly?

## Craft

- [ ] Any caption, label or line that repeats what the page already shows? Remove it.
- [ ] Any AI tells left: chapter numbers, "next" bars, arrows as decoration, dashes, triads?
- [ ] Any empty space or crowding a designer would notice?

Report the fixes as a short table: what would bother them, where, what you changed.
